Orato is built for multilingual markets like India. An assistant can start in one language and follow the caller if they switch.

## Supported languages

Choose the **Primary language** on the [Voice](/assistants/voice-and-language#languages) tab. Options include English (US), English (India), Hindi, Tamil, Telugu, Marathi, Gujarati, and Bengali.

## Set up a multilingual assistant

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick the primary language">
    On the **Voice** tab, set **Primary language** to the language most callers use, for example **Hindi**.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Turn on auto switch">
    Turn on **Auto switch language** so the assistant follows callers who change language mid-call.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a matching voice">
    Pick a voice that speaks the language naturally. **Sarvam** (Bulbul v3) is designed for Indian languages; Cartesia also offers Indian voices. Click **Preview** to check.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Translate the instructions">
    On the **Prompt** tab, click **Translate prompt**. Orato rewrites your instructions in the native script with a natural spoken tone, keeps placeholders like `{customer_name}` intact, and preserves your sections.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Check the transcriber">
    Orato picks a transcriber from your languages. To force one, open **Advanced → Transcriber → Change** and choose **Sarvam AI** (Saaras v4 Realtime) for Indian languages or **Deepgram Flux Multilingual** for mixed-language calls.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Consider the AI model">
    Most models handle Hindi well. **Sarvam 105B** is built for Indian languages and is worth testing for regional-language calls.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Tips for natural Indian-language calls

- Write instructions the way people actually speak on calls. Modern terms like "profile", "calendar", or "EMI" can stay in English or be transliterated.
- Tell the assistant how to handle Hinglish, for example `If the caller mixes Hindi and English, reply the same way.`
- Keep numbers simple. Ask the assistant to say amounts in lakhs and crores if that's what callers expect.
- Test with native speakers before launching a campaign.
