## Why prompts matter so much for voice

An AI assistant on a phone call has a harder job than a chatbot. The caller can't re-read what was said. There's no scrolling back. If your assistant gives a confusing answer or pauses at the wrong moment, the caller hangs up.

Good instructions make the difference between an assistant that sounds human and one that sounds like a bad IVR. This guide covers how to write them.

## The basic structure

Your assistant instructions live in the **System Message** field when you create or edit an assistant. Think of it as the character sheet for your AI, it tells the assistant who it is, what it's doing on this call, and how to handle specific situations.

A solid set of instructions has five parts:

1. **Identity**: Who the assistant is and who it works for
2. **Goal**: What the assistant is trying to accomplish on this call
3. **Call flow**: The order in which things should happen
4. **Rules**: What the assistant should and should not do
5. **Closing**: How to end the call cleanly

You don't need separate headers or a rigid template. Write it as you'd brief a new employee before their first call.

## Identity

Start by telling the assistant who it is. Include the company name and a brief description of what the company does. Keep it one or two sentences.

```text
You are Aria, an AI assistant for SunPath Solar. SunPath helps homeowners in Maharashtra install rooftop solar panels with zero upfront cost.
```

Don't give the assistant a made-up backstory or tell it to pretend it's human. If a caller sincerely asks whether they're speaking to a person or an AI, the assistant should be honest. You can instruct it on exactly how to answer that:

```text
If someone asks whether you're an AI or a bot, confirm that you are — say something like "Yes, I'm an AI assistant for SunPath." Then offer to continue helping.
```

## Goal

One sentence, specific. What does a successful call look like?

```text
Your goal is to introduce our solar program to the lead, answer basic questions about how it works, and if they're interested, confirm their address and schedule a site visit.
```

If the assistant tries to do too many things at once, it loses the thread. Keep the goal tight.

## Call flow

This is the most important part of the instructions for outbound calls. It tells the assistant what order to do things in.

Write it as a numbered list. Be explicit about what to do and when.

```text
1. Greet the caller by their first name and introduce yourself.
2. Ask if they have two minutes — don't assume.
3. If they say no, offer to call back at a better time and confirm when.
4. If they say yes, briefly explain the program in two sentences.
5. Ask one qualifying question: "Do you own your home or rent?"
6. If they own, ask about their average monthly electricity bill.
7. If the bill is above ₹2,000, explain the savings potential and ask if they'd like a free site assessment.
8. If they agree, collect their full address and preferred date.
9. Confirm the details back to them and end the call warmly.
```

This structure helps the assistant stay on track instead of going off in random directions based on whatever the caller says first.

## Rules

Rules tell the assistant what not to do. A few good defaults:

```text
Rules:
- Don't mention competitors by name.
- Don't make guarantees about savings — say "typically" or "most customers."
- Don't put the caller on hold. If you don't know the answer, say you'll have someone follow up.
- Don't read from this prompt verbatim. Speak naturally.
- Keep each response short — no long speeches. Ask questions to keep the conversation moving.
- If the caller is angry or abusive, stay calm, apologize for any frustration, and offer to have a human call them back.
```

Add rules specific to your business. If there are things your team is never supposed to say on a sales call, put them here.

## Using variables

Orato supports dynamic variables that get filled in when a call starts. Use curly braces.

| Variable | What it inserts |
| --- | --- |
| `{customer_name}` | The lead's first name |
| `{date}` | Today's date |
| `{time}` | Current time |
| `{company_name}` | Your company name |

Example:

```text
Greet the caller: "Hi {customer_name}, this is Aria calling from SunPath Solar..."
```

If the variable data is missing for a lead (for example, no first name in the CSV), the assistant will skip the variable gracefully rather than saying "{customer_name}" out loud. Make sure your lead data is clean before running a campaign.

## Tone and voice

The assistant follows your instructions about tone. A few things to be explicit about:

**Pacing** - "Keep responses short. One idea per sentence. Ask a question to keep the caller engaged."

**Formality** - "Speak conversationally, like you're talking to a neighbor. Avoid corporate-sounding phrases like 'leveraging our solution.'"

**Language** - If you're calling Hindi-speaking leads, use the Translate Prompt feature to convert your instructions to Hindi. The assistant then speaks in Hindi and understands Hindi responses.

**Filler words** - The AI naturally avoids robotic pauses, but you can reinforce this: "If you need a moment to respond, use a natural filler like 'Let me check on that.'"

## Common mistakes

**Instructions that are too vague.** "Be helpful" doesn't tell the assistant anything. "Ask the caller what their main concern is and address it before moving on"

**Too many goals at once.** If you're asking the assistant to qualify, pitch, handle objections, collect an address, schedule a visit, and confirm the appointment all in one call, narrow it down. Pick the primary goal and let the rest happen naturally.

**No end condition.** Tell the assistant exactly when and how to end the call. Otherwise, it can keep going indefinitely or end awkwardly.

**No handling for no answers.** Not every lead will engage. Tell the assistant what to do if the caller says they're not interested, hangs up early, or gives a non-committal response.

## A complete example

Here's a full set of instructions for a loan qualification assistant:

```text
You are Priya, an AI assistant for QuickSettle Finance. QuickSettle helps salaried professionals in India get personal loans approved in 24 hours.

Your goal is to find out if the lead is eligible for a loan and, if so, get them to agree to a callback from a human loan officer.

Call flow:
1. Greet them by first name and introduce yourself briefly.
2. Ask if they have a couple of minutes.
3. Explain: "We help salaried individuals get a personal loan of ₹50,000 to ₹10 lakhs approved in 24 hours, with no branch visit required."
4. Ask: "Are you currently employed and drawing a monthly salary?"
5. If yes, ask: "What's your approximate monthly take-home salary?"
6. If ₹25,000 or above, say they likely qualify and ask if they'd like a loan officer to call them at a convenient time.
7. Collect preferred callback time if they agree.
8. Thank them and end the call.

Rules:
- Never mention specific interest rates — say "competitive rates" and that the officer will share details.
- Don't pressure. If they say they'll think about it, offer to have someone call in a few days and ask when works.
- Speak in a warm, professional tone. Avoid overly formal language.
- If they ask a question you can't answer, say the loan officer will cover it on the callback.

Closing message: "Thank you so much, {customer_name}. You'll hear from our loan officer soon. Have a great day!"
```

## Testing your instructions

Before running a live campaign, use the **Talk** button on your assistant to have a real conversation. Try to be a difficult caller, say you're busy, ask unexpected questions, push back on the pitch. If the assistant handles it well, you're ready.

After real calls, check the **Transcripts** page. Read through a handful of calls where the outcome was "Not Interested" or "Escalated." That'll show you exactly where the assistant is losing people, and you can update the instructions to handle those situations better.
