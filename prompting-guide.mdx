## Why instructions matter

Your AI Assistant is only as good as the instructions you give it. The LLM is powerful, but it needs direction, who it is, what the call is about, how to handle specific situations. A vague prompt produces a vague assistant. A clear, specific prompt produces one that sounds like it was trained for the job.

This guide covers how to write instructions that actually work.

---

## The basic structure

Every good instruction prompt has three parts:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Identity: who the assistant is">
    Tell the assistant its name, company, and role. Keep it short.

    ```text
        # IDENTITY
            You are Arya, a sales assistant for Orato. 
                You help businesses discover how AI calling can grow their revenue.
                    You're warm, confident, and get to the point.
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Voice rules: how to speak">
    This is the most important section for calls. Without it, your assistant will sound like a chatbot.

    ```text
        # CRITICAL RULE: VOICE-FIRST RESPONSES
            Every response must be 1-2 sentences maximum.
                You are speaking out loud — never use bullet points, 
                    asterisks, emojis, or any formatting.
                        Sound like a confident, helpful human.
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Goal: what to do on the call">
    Tell the assistant exactly what it's trying to achieve, what questions to ask, and how to end the call.

    ```text
        # YOUR GOAL
            Your job is to qualify leads for the sales team.
                Ask: company size, current challenge, and budget range.
                    If they're a good fit, offer to book a demo.
                        If they're not interested, thank them and end the call politely.
    ```
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Rules for writing good prompts

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Keep responses short, always">
    The number one mistake is writing instructions that produce long responses. On a phone call, anything longer than two sentences sounds like a monologue. The caller will either interrupt or hang up.

    **Bad:**

    ```text
        Provide a comprehensive overview of all the features 
            and benefits of our product, including pricing tiers, 
                integrations, and support options.
    ```

    **Good:**

    ```text
        Give a one-sentence summary of what we do, 
            then ask what they're looking for.
    ```
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Be specific about the goal">
    Don't say "qualify the lead." Say exactly what qualifies them.

    **Vague:**

    ```text
        Qualify the lead and see if they're a good fit.
    ```

    **Specific:**

    ```text
        A lead is qualified if: they have a team of 5+ salespeople, 
            they're currently spending time on manual outbound calls, 
                and they have a budget above ₹30,000/month.
    ```
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Tell it what NOT to do">
    Explicitly stating what to avoid is just as important as telling it what to do.

    ```text
        Never discuss competitor pricing.
            Never promise features that aren't confirmed.
                Never keep talking if the caller says they're busy — 
                    offer to call back instead.
    ```
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Handle the most common objections">
    Think about the top 3 things callers say that derail a call, and write a response for each.

    ```text
        If they say "I'm busy right now":
            Apologise, ask for a better time, and offer to schedule a callback.
    
                If they say "We already use a similar tool":
                    Ask what they use and what they like about it before continuing.
    
                        If they say "Just send me an email":
                            Agree, confirm their email address, and still try to get one 
                                qualifying question answered before ending the call.
    ```
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Write a clear ending">
    Tell your assistant exactly how and when to wrap up the call.

    ```text
        # ENDING THE CALL
            Once you have answers to all three qualifying questions, 
                summarise what you heard in one sentence and offer 
                    to connect them with the team. 
                        Always thank them before hanging up.
    ```
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>
