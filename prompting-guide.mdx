Your assistant is only as good as its instructions. A clear, specific prompt produces an assistant that sounds trained for the job. A vague one produces a vague assistant.

<Tip>
  Short on time? Use **Generate** on the [Prompt](/assistants/prompt) tab or start from a [template](/assistant-templates), then refine with this guide.
</Tip>

## Use sections

Type `/` at the start of a line in **Instructions** to insert a section. A good prompt usually has these:

<Steps>
  <Step title="Identity">
    Who the agent is and who they represent.

    ```text
    # Identity
    You are Priya, a calling assistant for Acme Realty, a property consultancy in Mumbai.
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Objective">
    What a successful call looks like.

    ```text
    # Objective
    Qualify leads who showed interest in buying a home. If they are serious,
    book a site visit or a callback with a human agent.
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Speaking style">
    The most important section for voice. Without it, assistants sound like chatbots.

    ```text
    # Speaking style
    Speak in clear, warm Indian English. Keep every reply to one or two short sentences.
    Never use lists, symbols, or emojis. Ask one question at a time.
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Steps">
    The call flow.

    ```text
    # Steps
    1. Confirm they are still looking for property.
    2. Ask whether they want to buy or rent, and the property type.
    3. Ask for preferred locations and budget.
    4. Ask about timeline.
    5. If serious, offer a site visit. If not, thank them and end the call.
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Guardrails">
    Hard rules.

    ```text
    # Guardrails
    Never quote exact prices unless they are in your knowledge.
    Never invent project names, RERA numbers, or offers.
    If the caller asks not to be called again, apologize and end the call.
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Error handling">
    What to do when things go off script.

    ```text
    # Error handling
    If you did not understand, ask the caller to repeat once.
    If the caller is silent, ask "Are you still there?" once.
    If the caller goes off topic, answer briefly and return to the next step.
    ```
  </Step>
  <Step title="Context">
    Background facts: company details, offers, office hours, FAQs. For long material, use the [knowledge base](/knowledge-base) instead.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Rules that make a difference

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Keep replies short">
    On a call, anything longer than two sentences sounds like a monologue. Callers interrupt or hang up.

    **Avoid:** "Explain all features, pricing tiers, and integrations."

    **Better:** "Give a one-sentence summary of what we do, then ask what they need."
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Define success precisely">
    **Vague:** "Qualify the lead."

    **Specific:** "A lead is qualified if they plan to buy within 3 months and their budget is above ₹80 lakh."
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Say what not to do">
    Explicit limits are as important as goals: no competitor talk, no promises you can't keep, no pushing a busy caller.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Script the top objections">
    ```text
    If they say they're busy: apologize and ask for a better time to call back.
    If they say "just send me an email": confirm their email, then ask one qualifying question.
    If they say they already have a solution: ask what they like about it before continuing.
    ```
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Use variables">
    Insert `{{customer_name}}` with the `@` shortcut so every call feels personal.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Plan the hand-off">
    If you set a [Transfer to Human](/assistants/advanced#transfer-to-human) number, tell the assistant exactly when to use it and what to say first.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Match the language">
    If callers speak Hindi or a regional language, translate the instructions with **Translate prompt**. See [Multilingual assistants](/multilingual).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Test and iterate

<Steps>
  <Step title="Talk to it">
    Use **Talk** to run realistic test calls, including difficult callers.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review conversations">
    Open [Conversations](/conversations) and read where the assistant went wrong.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Refine one thing at a time">
    Use **Generate & refine** or edit the section directly, then test again.
  </Step>
</Steps>
