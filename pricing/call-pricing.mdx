## How call costs are calculated

Every call's credit consumption comes from what actually happened on that call. There are three components, and they're all metered independently:

**Voice AI**: The speech processing that drives the conversation. This includes transcribing what the caller says, generating a response, and synthesizing that response back into speech.

**Telephony**: The cost of connecting the call over the phone network. This varies by the country you're calling and the direction of the call (inbound vs. outbound).

**Platform fee**: A flat per-minute charge on top of your provider costs, covering the Orato infrastructure that coordinates everything.

You only pay for what they actually consumed. If a call drops in 10 seconds, you're charged for 10 seconds, not a full minute.

---

## The three Voice AI components

Voice AI breaks into three sub-components, each billed by a different unit.

### Speech-to-Text (STT)

The transcriber converts the caller's speech into text so the AI can understand it. Billed by call duration, rounded to the second.

Your choice of STT provider and model affects this cost. Orato supports multiple providers — lighter models cost less.

### Language Model (LLM)

The LLM processes the transcribed text and generates a response. Billed by tokens — the number of words and punctuation pieces it reads and writes.

Longer conversations, more complex prompts, and larger context windows all drive token usage up. A concise, well-structured prompt is the single biggest lever you have on LLM cost.

### Text-to-Speech (TTS)

The TTS engine converts the AI's text response back into spoken audio. Billed by the number of characters synthesized.

Provider and voice selection both affect this rate. Neural voices tend to cost more than standard ones.

---

## Telephony costs

Telephony is billed by call duration, rounded to the nearest minute. The rate depends on:

- **Direction** — inbound calls (people calling your number) and outbound calls (Orato dialing out) are priced differently
- **Country** — local calls within the same country cost less than cross-border calls

You can see estimated telephony rates for your target countries in your dashboard before launching a campaign.

## Viewing your call costs

After every conversation, open **Call History** and select any call to see a full credit breakdown — exactly how many credits each component consumed. This makes it straightforward to spot which component is driving cost and tune accordingly.

<Tip>
  If you notice LLM costs running high, the first thing to check is your system prompt length. Redundant instructions add tokens to every single call.
</Tip>

---

## Reducing your per-call cost

A few practical levers:

**Choose lighter models where quality allows.** A smaller, faster LLM is often sufficient for structured tasks like appointment booking or FAQ handling. Reserve heavier models for complex conversations.

**Keep prompts tight.** Every word in your system prompt is re-read on every call. Trim anything that doesn't directly shape behaviour.

**Use the Knowledge Base.** Putting facts in the Knowledge Base lets the AI retrieve them on demand instead of keeping them in the main prompt — often reducing token usage significantly.

**Test before you launch.** Pro\+ includes free voice and audio testing so you can hear how your assistant sounds and catch prompt issues before spending credits on live calls.

---

## Credits vs. subscription

Credits are always separate from your subscription.

Your plan (Free, Pro, Pro\+) determines your features and capacity. Credits cover usage — what actually happened on calls. Buying more credits doesn't change your plan, and upgrading your plan doesn't give you a credit balance.

<Info>
  Credits never expire on any plan. See [Pricing Overview](/pricing/overview) for plan details and subscription pricing.
</Info>
